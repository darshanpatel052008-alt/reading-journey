My Reading Timeline

From cutting up comics to choosing my own books, every chapter changed how I saw reading.

01
AROUND AGE SEVEN · CHICAGO

Discovering Spider-Man

A neighbor who collected comic books gave my parents a stack of Spider-Man comics. At first, I was more interested in the pictures and even cut up some pages like a collage. Once I actually started reading them, I learned how the pictures and speech bubbles worked together. Peter Parker felt like someone from an entirely different world that I could get to know.

MY FIRST STEP INTO READING
02
FIFTH GRADE

Where Are the Pictures?

My younger sister was reading Harry Potter, and I got curious enough to try it myself. My first reaction was wondering where all the pictures had gone. Imagining Dumbledore, baby Harry, and a flying motorcycle was harder than following comic panels. But eventually I realized I could create my own version of the story in my head.

THE MOVIE MOVED INTO MY HEAD
03
GROWING UP

More Characters, More Worlds

After Spider-Man, I started reading about characters like Daredevil and The Punisher. Around age thirteen, those comics even led me to watch television shows about the characters. Reading became more than just a way to pass time. It was a way to explore other lives and stories across different formats.

04
SEVENTH–EIGHTH GRADE · COVID

When Reading Felt Like a Chore

During COVID, I went through a reading slump. The books assigned in school weren't always bad, but a lot of them just didn't interest me. With so little say in what I read, reading started feeling more like homework than something I actually enjoyed. For a while, I distanced myself from it.

PAUSED, NOT FINISHED.

05
JUNIOR YEAR OF HIGH SCHOOL

Finding My Way Back

I came across a video about becoming a reader instead of forcing yourself to read. That idea stuck with me, so I started using Chicago's library system and picking books that actually interested me. Stephen King became one of my favorite authors, especially It and The Long Walk. For the first time in a while, reading felt like my own choice again.

06
FIRST YEAR OF COLLEGE · TODAY

The Story Is Still Going

Now that I'm in college, I have more freedom to explore libraries, bookstores, and people with similar interests. I still read comics sometimes, but novels have become a bigger part of my reading life. In my original essay, I was reading Stephen King's Billy Summers. More importantly, I've learned that choosing what to read can make reading meaningful again.
